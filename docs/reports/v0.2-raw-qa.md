### The bar count is short of the target, and cleaning will widen the gap

The roadmap asks for **~50 M clean bars**. The tier holds **42.36 M raw**, about 15 % below, and
v0.3's cleaning pass removes bars rather than adding them — so the honest reading is that the
distance grows from here rather than closes.

The shortfall is diffuse rather than one missing thing. Against the plan's own estimates:

| Source | Estimated | Actual | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Crypto | ~15 M | 10.89 M | −4.11 M |
| Dukascopy | ~4 M | 3.66 M | −0.34 M |
| Equities | 30–60 M | 27.77 M | −2.23 M below the low end |

Every source came in at or under the bottom of its range, and the cause is the same in each
case: **average history per series is shorter than the estimate assumed.** The 200 spot pairs
average about 4.2 years of hourly history, not the near-decade the top of the range implies;
the 12 425 equity series average 2 235 daily bars, about 8.9 years.

The three ways to close it are all things an ADR has already excluded from this version:

- **Crypto 15m and 5m** would add 50–150 M bars. That is corpus M1, and ADR-0006 gates it at G3
  precisely so the decision is made against a trained tokenizer rather than a spreadsheet.
- **A wider Binance universe.** ADR-0011 pins top-200 spot and top-100 um by a stated metric.
  Widening it to hit a bar count would make the universe a function of the target rather than of
  liquidity.
- **Dukascopy index CFDs.** ADR-0015 declines them: the corpus already has equity exposure with
  real share volume and a real exchange calendar.

So this is recorded as a **shortfall, not a pass**. 42.36 M bars across four asset classes and
two frequencies is enough to train the 25 M-parameter model v0.7 specifies — Kronos trained on
12 B bars, and the honesty banner already says this corpus is small by comparison — but the
number is under target and saying otherwise would be the first dishonest line in the project.

## What each source cost to land

### Binance — unchanged from v0.1

600 series, 10.89 M bars, untouched this version. The v0.1 sidecars were re-read by v0.2 code and
every recorded `manifest_sha256` still matched its own fields, which is what the backward-
compatible manifest extension was for.

### Dukascopy — 52 of 54 items

26 of 27 instruments at both frequencies. **NZDJPY is absent at 1h and 1d**: the vendor published
bars with `high` below `open`, which ADR-0010 refuses rather than repairs. That is a coverage
fact, recorded here rather than left to be noticed.

Weekend padding is real and counted, not removed. Some eras of the feed carry synthetic flat bars
across the weekend — in May 2012, 88 of 96 weekend hours have OHLC all equal to the Friday close
with volume exactly 0.0. `closed_window_count` in each manifest sizes it, and v0.3's
consecutive-stagnant-run filter is what will drop it.

### Stooq — 12 422 of 12 452 series

867 tickers were skipped as too short (< 30 rows) and recorded as such. **30 series were rejected
on invariants** — 0.24 % of files — where the vendor published `high < low` or `low > close`.
Those parsed correctly; they are refused because the bars cannot be true.

The parse-failure rate proper is **0 %**. The two numbers are reported separately because
conflating them gates the run on the vendor's data quality and calls it a parser problem.

### Yahoo — 503 of 503 tickers

50 694 split and dividend events, zero failures. Loader-only redistribution class: the events are
cached privately and never republished.

## Reachability, measured

Three carried unverifieds from the plan, all resolved by asking rather than assuming. Two came
out the opposite of what was expected.

| Question | Expected | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| Dukascopy from a cloud runner | unknown | **403 on GitHub Actions**, works on Kaggle |
| Stooq URL from another IP | possibly IP-bound | **404 everywhere within minutes** — short-lived, not IP-bound |
| Yahoo blocking cloud IPs | expected to block | **20/20 tickers, zero failures** from Actions |

The Dukascopy result moved that one pull to Kaggle permanently (ADR-0015). The Stooq result
matters more than it looks: 404 means expired-or-single-use, while 403 would have meant IP-bound,
and only the second is what ADR-0016's laptop staging exception exists for. Reaching for the
exception on a 404 would have broken the zero-bytes rule to solve a problem it does not solve.

## Storage and infrastructure

**1.90 GB against the 100 GB free tier** — the headroom check passes by a factor of fifty. The
equities tier is 62 % of it.

`axiom-raw` gained no new infrastructure: no new repo, no new service, no new account. What
changed instead is which backend runs which job, and that was forced by measurement rather than
chosen.

Two limits were found the expensive way and are now documented in `docs/RUNBOOK.md`:

- **The Hub allows 128 commits an hour per repository.** At 50 files a commit, the equities tier
  is ~500 commits and the first attempt died partway. At 2 000 files a commit it is ~13, and the
  second attempt finished in 49 minutes with zero Hub errors.
- **The Hub rate-limits reads too, and not uniformly.** Small files retry internally; large ones
  go through Xet, whose 429s arrive as an unretried exception. The registry read all 13 580
  sidecars through 162 rate-limit warnings without losing one; the universe ranking, reading
  Parquet, lost most of its input to the same pressure and had to be rebuilt.

**GitHub Actions minutes ran out** partway through the version, and the repository was made public
in response (ADR-0017). Public repos get unlimited standard-runner minutes. The source was always
publishable under `DATA_LICENSING.md`; `axiom-raw` stays private permanently.

## Zero bytes on the laptop

Held. No market data was resident on the laptop or the home PC at any point in v0.2, and the
ADR-0016 staging exception **was not used** — `staging_exception_used` is false in every pull run
manifest. The Stooq archive was downloaded by the runner from a URL a human handed over; what
crossed the laptop was a URL, which is not market data.

The one moment it came close is worth recording: a browser download of the 516 MB archive was
started and cancelled during the URL handoff. Nothing was retained.

## Invariants

Zero violations in the tier, by construction: `validate_bars(..., raise_on_error=True)` runs on
every series before it is written, so a file that broke an invariant is not present to be counted.
The 30 Stooq series and 2 Dukascopy series that failed it are absent, and named above.

Two checks became warnings this version, both because the first version of the rule was wrong
about real data:

- **Off-grid bars** (v0.1) — real bars on a shifted phase after an exchange restart.
- **Weekend bars in a 24×5 session** (v0.2) — an earlier reopen in 2003, and vendor padding.
  Asserting this rejected 24 of 27 hourly FX series on their first real pull.

Both are counted in the manifests. A raw tier that rejects a vendor convention is a cleaning pass
that nobody documented, and v0.3's drop statistics would then be measured against a baseline that
does not exist.
