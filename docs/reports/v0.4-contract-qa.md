## What passed

**The causality audit, on real bars.** 300 of 300 split points across 50 random segments reproduce
bit-exactly: `transform(bars[:t+1])` is the first `t` rows of `transform(bars)`, same last digit.
The CI property battery proves the arithmetic on generated series; this closes the loop on real ones
- weekend gaps, stale runs, phase-shifted Binance hours and all - without moving a byte to the
laptop.

**Zero NaN or Inf** across 77.8 million feature rows. A NaN out of valid input would be a bug in the
contract rather than a property of the data, and the transform checks for one before it returns.

**The usable-window correction.** Under the anchor-bar rule the corpus holds **27,492,792**
context-512 windows, against the 27,508,145 the v0.3 report published. The difference is 15,353,
which is one per segment: bar 0 produces no feature row. The v0.3 table is superseded by this one.

**Throughput: 6,950 bars/sec/core.** Two orders of magnitude under the plan's 1 M target, and
adequate - v0.6 pre-tokenizes one spec over 38.8 M bars, which is under two core-hours and trivially
parallel. `bottleneck.move_median` remains the documented upgrade path, and ADR-0020 carries the
arithmetic to re-check it against if corpus M1 lands.

**Cost: $0.** Two corpus passes on GitHub Actions runners, which are unlimited on a public repo
(ADR-0017). Zero Modal credits, zero Kaggle GPU-hours, zero market-data bytes on the laptop.
## Red flags
### The investigation the gate asks for

**This is the second fit.** The first one clipped **19.2 %** of the FX daily gap feature, 15.5 % of
FX daily volume, and over 0.5 % on 76 of the 84 combinations - and the cause was the estimator, not
the data. `scale` was IQR/1.349, and a daily FX bar opens where the previous one closed, near enough
that the middle 50 % of the gap feature spans about 2.5e-5. IQR was measuring the width of a spike
at zero and reporting it as the scale of the feature, which put every real gap tens of sigma out.
The fix, in ADR-0020, floors the scale with `(q99 - q1)/4.6527`: the same normal-consistent
normalization one quantile pair further out, so on a well-behaved feature the two agree and the
floor does nothing.

It worked. The worst slice went from 19.222 % to 0.896 %, and the scaled 1-99 span now sits at
4.6-5.0 against a normal's 4.65 - the scaling is doing what it claims to.

**What is left is the market, not the fit, and it is not being tuned away.** Thirty-six combinations
remain, between 0.513 % and 0.896 %, and they share a shape: **the one-sided features dominate.**
`upper` is `log(high/open)` and is never negative; `lower` is never positive. Scaling a half-
distribution by a symmetric spread puts the entire short side into half a unit - commodity daily
`lower` reaches only +0.5148 at its 99.9th percentile - and spends the whole clip budget on one
tail. The rest are `gap` and the flow features, where genuine leptokurtosis puts more mass past five
sigma than a normal has anywhere.

Widening the estimator again would push these under 0.5 %, and it is refused. The 1-99 floor had a
reason: the centre was degenerate, so the estimator was measuring the wrong thing. There is no such
reason here - the centre is fine and the tails are fat, which is what financial returns are.
Widening further would be choosing a constant to satisfy a threshold, and there would be no
principled place to stop.

**So this is v0.5's problem, and it is the most useful thing this report carries.** The wick
features want an asymmetric quantizer range; a symmetric one spends most of its levels on a side of
the axis `lower` can never reach. Quantizer level and range selection is a v0.4 non-goal by design,
and this is the measurement it was deferred to.

### What v0.5 should take from this

**Nothing in this table separates the two parameterizations.** Clip rates agree to within 0.11
percentage points in every slice, and the 1-99 spans to within 0.01 except commodity daily. That is
the intended result: the specs were built to be identical in every respect a consumer can see, so
the A/B measures the parameterization rather than an incidental difference in scaling.

The one place they differ is equity daily, where `geo-v1` clips 0.540 % against `ret-v1`'s 0.428 %.
`geo-v1` splits a bar into a gap plus a body, and US equities gap overnight, so the gap carries more
of the move and its tail is heavier. Whether that is a cost or the point is what the reconstruction
A/B decides - a representation that separates the overnight jump from the session's move may
reconstruct worse and predict better.

No winner is picked here. The survivor is decided by tokenizer reconstruction in v0.5, on held-out
pre-firewall data, not by which distribution reads more tidily.
